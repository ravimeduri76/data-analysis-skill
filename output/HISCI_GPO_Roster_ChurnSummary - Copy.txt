  HISCI-HealthTrust Roster — Survival & Churn Analysis

  Domain: Healthcare membership network — 4,996 member organizations analysed. Note: domain detected by keyword heuristic; this is a
  GPO (Group Purchasing Organization) / healthcare network roster, not a clinical dataset. The GPO-specific lenses (contract value,
  NPI linkage) are more relevant than clinical metrics.

  ---
  What the data represents

  Each row is a healthcare provider organization (hospital, ambulatory practice, home care agency, etc.) whose event is defined as
  leaving the HealthTrust network (Inactive status). Active members are right-censored at the analysis date (2026-03-25) — they've
  been observed up to today but haven't churned yet.

  ---
  Key Findings

  1. The network is highly sticky overall

  ┌──────────┬────────────────┐
  │ Horizon  │ % Still Active │
  ├──────────┼────────────────┤
  │ 1 year   │ 96.6%          │
  ├──────────┼────────────────┤
  │ 3 years  │ 89.9%          │
  ├──────────┼────────────────┤
  │ 5 years  │ 81.4%          │
  ├──────────┼────────────────┤
  │ 10 years │ 65.0%          │
  └──────────┴────────────────┘

  Median membership survival = 7,154 days (~19.6 years). Half of all member organizations that ever joined are still members after
  nearly two decades — strong retention by any standard.

  2. Hazard is constant over time (Weibull rho = 0.996 ≈ 1)

  The Weibull model fits decisively better than LogNormal (ΔAIC = 155.4), and its shape parameter is essentially 1 — meaning churn
  risk is flat and memoryless. There is no "honeymoon period" of high early churn, nor a wear-out effect as tenure increases. A
  member who has been in the network for 2 years has the same monthly probability of leaving as one who just joined. This is
  characteristic of large institutional membership networks where exits are driven by structural events (acquisition, bankruptcy,
  budget cuts), not by dissatisfaction that accumulates over time.

  3. Class of Trade is the dominant churn driver

  Home Care and Ambulatory Care MD organizations churn at roughly 2.8× and 2.6× the rate of the average member — highly statistically
   significant (p < 0.0001 and p < 0.001 respectively). These segments likely face more volatile business conditions: home care
  agencies are sensitive to reimbursement policy changes; physician-owned MD practices face consolidation pressure.

  4. Non-Profit status is protective; Unknown org status is a risk signal

  - Non-Profit organizations (921 active) churn at 15.2% lower hazard than For-Profit (HR=0.848, p=0.009). Institutional stability
  and mission-driven membership likely explains this.
  - Members with Unknown org status (455 active) churn at 19.9% higher hazard (HR=1.199, p=0.004) — this is partly a data quality
  signal: incomplete member profiles correlate with weaker engagement and higher exit risk.

  5. Cohort effect: newer joiners are stickier

  Each calendar year a member joins later, their churn hazard is 2.5% lower (HR=0.975 per year, p < 0.0001). Members who joined in
  2020 have about 25% lower hazard than those who joined in 2010. This may reflect: (a) improved onboarding/engagement programs in
  recent years; (b) newer members joining for more strategic reasons; or (c) survivorship selection — early leavers among recent
  cohorts haven't had time to churn yet, so the true cohort effect may be partially illusory. Worth monitoring.

  6. Region has no significant effect on churn

  None of the four US Census regions reached p < 0.05. Churn is driven by care type and org status, not geography.
